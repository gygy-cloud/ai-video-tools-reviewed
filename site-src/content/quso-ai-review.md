## What quso.ai Actually Is

quso.ai takes one long video — a podcast episode, a webinar, a stream, a sermon — and cuts it into short vertical clips with animated captions and automatic reframing. The pitch is volume: one recording becomes a week of posts without an editor.

The product has expanded well past clipping: an AI subtitle generator covering 99 languages, a content planner, a scheduler for seven networks, social analytics, filler-word removal, scene-change detection and a b-roll library. Usage is metered in **credits, where one credit is roughly one minute of processed video**.

The rebrand matters if you have ever searched for this tool. **vidyo.ai is now quso.ai** — same company (Vidyoai Internet Ventures Inc, per the affiliate terms), same team, bigger product. Existing vidyo.ai affiliates keep their dashboard and commissions; new affiliates join one program branded "Friends of quso.ai," so an old vidyo.ai affiliate page in a search result points at the same place.

The affiliate page claims **4 million creators** use the product. That is vendor-reported with no independent source in the pages reviewed — marketing copy, not a verified user count.

## The 30% Is Not 30% of the Sticker Price

Most reviews of this program miss this, and it is the single most important number for anyone planning to promote it.

quso.ai's affiliate page runs an earnings calculator: **10 paid referrals on Essential at $39/month → $117/month, $1,404/year.** The arithmetic is correct — $390 × 30% = $117 — but it assumes every referral is on **monthly billing, at list price, before tax**.

Section 2.1 of the affiliate terms defines the actual base:

> "You earn 30% of the net amount we receive from every payment... **The net amount is what the customer paid after discounts, less taxes and payment-processing fees, converted to US dollars.**"

Three subtractions sit between the sticker price and your commission:

1. **Discounts** — including the 50% annual discount, any promotion, and any code the customer used.
2. **Taxes** — the pricing page itself says tax may be added at checkout; tax is removed before your percentage applies.
3. **Payment-processing fees** — the card or payment-rail fee on the transaction.

The affiliate page does disclose this, in small type under the calculator: *"Commission is calculated after taxes and payment fees, so actual earnings are a little lower."* It is honest. It is also easy to miss, and "a little lower" is doing quiet work once the annual discount enters the picture.

## The 30-Day Cookie Against a Free Tier That Lasts a Month

The attribution window is **30 days, last-click** (section 2.2). A click that converts within 30 days is yours; a later click from someone else's link takes it.

Thirty days is mid-pack — the same as Zebracat and Vid.AI, shorter than StoryLab and DreamWild at 90. For a clipping tool the window interacts with a specific behaviour: **people try before they buy, and the free tier is sized to let them try for a while.** At 75 credits a month, a creator with one weekly podcast can sample the product for weeks without paying. If the upgrade lands on day 34, the cookie is gone.

Two terms work in your favour here:

- **Partner codes credit without a click.** Section 2.2: *"A partner code we issue to you credits you when a customer uses it, with or without a click."* Most programs in this niche are cookie-only. A code is the practical fix for the long-trial problem — put it on screen in a video or in a newsletter, and attribution no longer depends on a browser holding a cookie for a month.
- **Free signups are tracked even though they pay nothing.** Section 2.3 confirms no commission on free plans, but the affiliate page adds that referred free signups stay visible in your dashboard, so you can see who is sitting in the trial and follow up while the relationship is still warm.

The counterweight is section 2.7, which is unusually blunt: *"We are not responsible for referrals lost to cookie blocking, ad blockers, browser settings or technical failures."* Standard language, but worth knowing before you build a plan around cookie-only tracking.

## The Terms That Actually Constrain You

The program is auto-approval — the affiliate page says *"no approval hoops, no minimum audience size,"* and the directory lists approval as automatic. That is a genuine advantage over OpusClip, which requires manual review. The restrictions are where the friction lives.

**Paid ads are blocked, not just banned.** Section 3.1: *"Clicks from paid ads are blocked and do not earn commission."* That is technical enforcement — every click you buy is a click that will not track.

**Brand-term advertising is prohibited across a wide net.** Section 3.2 forbids bidding on or using in ad text: quso, quso.ai, quso ai, vidyo, vidyo.ai, vidyo ai, their misspellings, and any of those combined with words like *coupon, promo, discount, deal, **review**, **pricing**, login* or *alternative* — in any search, social or display network, *"even if the ad sends people to your own page first."* Note what this does and does not mean: it bans **ads** on "quso.ai review," not the **article**. Organic reviews and comparisons are explicitly allowed in section 3.

**Disclosure has a floor.** Section 4.1 requires wording your audience understands — *"I earn a commission if you buy through my link"* — and warns that a bare "affiliate link" label *"may not be enough."* Section 4.2 requires it next to the link, before the click, and spoken aloud in video and audio.

**Payouts: monthly on the 15th, $20 minimum, tax form required.** Section 6.1 targets the 15th for the previous month's commissions via PayPal in USD; section 6.2 confirms sub-$20 balances carry over and approved commissions do not expire. Section 6.3 is the one people miss: before the first payout you must supply a PayPal email **and** the correct US tax form — W-9 for US persons, **W-8BEN for non-US individuals**, W-8BEN-E for non-US businesses.

**Refunds reverse commission, including paid commission.** Section 5.1 reverses commission on refunded or disputed payments and deducts already-paid amounts from future commissions; section 5.2 allows a review of up to 90 days before payout; section 5.3 lets quso.ai withhold commission it reasonably believes came from fraud or a breach, with a written reason and 30 days to respond.

**"Lifetime" has an exit condition.** Section 7.3: when your account closes, for any reason, commission stops on payments made after the closing date. Section 7.4 protects your earned balance with a final payout even below $20, but the recurring stream ends with your account — so "for life" means "for as long as you stay in the program." Section 8.2 does require 14 days' notice before a rate cut, and section 8.3 guarantees changes never reduce commissions already earned.
