## What Vid.AI Actually Is

Vid.AI is a browser-based generator for people who publish video at volume without appearing on camera. The formats give it away: vertical Shorts, TikToks and Reels; 16:9 long-form for faceless YouTube channels; "text story" videos — fake text conversations over gameplay; and split-screen videos with a story on top of satisfying footage.

The pipeline is single-pass: you supply an idea, and it writes the script, generates a voiceover, picks stock footage, times captions to the words and lays music underneath. Every plan carries the same toolkit — tiers differ only in volume and which models you can reach. Two features are unusual at this price: **Animated Avatars** (a presenter in your own style that lip-syncs and gestures) and an **MCP server** for generating videos from Claude or ChatGPT.

That is the real tier gate, and the pricing page buries it. **Starter reaches Seedance 1 Pro Fast and Wan 2.2.** Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.5 and Kling 2.5 Turbo are marked "Unlock with Plus." If model quality is why you are buying, $39/month does not buy it.

## The Credit Math the Pricing Page Does Not Reconcile

The plan table sells **videos**. The FAQ talks about **credits**. These are not the same currency, and Vid.AI publishes the exchange rate for exactly one thing: avatars.

From the FAQ: creating an avatar costs **20 credits** (refunded if generation fails), and you can keep up to 20. Inside a video, an **intro** uses **10 credits**, an **outro** uses **10**, and **on-screen moments cost 5 credits for every 10% of the video they cover**.

Now price a one-minute short that opens with an avatar, closes with one, and keeps it on screen for half the runtime:

> 10 (intro) + 10 (outro) + 5 × 5 (50% on screen) = **45 credits per video**

Against the credit range the FAQ gives — **200 to 1,500 a month** — that is roughly **4 avatar-led videos on the low tier and 33 on the high tier**. Starter advertises **40 videos** a month. Pro advertises **300**.

The gap is about ten to one, and it is a *floor*: those 45 credits cover only avatar overhead, and the page never publishes a base per-video credit cost, so the real figure is higher. The advertised counts are almost certainly calculated with no avatars in play.

This is arithmetic from published per-avatar costs, not a measured benchmark, and the FAQ does not map credits to tiers, so I use the ends of the stated range as bookends. The direction still holds: **avatars are priced in the scarcer currency, and the plan table does not say so.**

## What Vid.AI Does Well

**The output is publish-ready, not a draft.** Script, voiceover, footage, captions and music arrive assembled. The value is not "AI magic" — it is deleting four steps from a workflow that otherwise takes an afternoon per video. Captions are timed to every word, and commercial use is explicitly allowed with royalty-free stock music.

**The format list matches how short-form actually works.** Text-story and split-screen are named as first-class outputs rather than bolted onto a generic generator. If your channel lives in one of those formats, that specificity beats a broader feature list.

## Where Vid.AI Falls Short

**No free trial, and the refund is narrower than it sounds.** Thirty days on *unused* credits means a subscription you actively used is largely non-refundable.

**Credit opacity is the biggest product problem.** The pricing page sells videos, the FAQ sells credits, and the only published conversion is for avatars. You cannot compute your cost per video before buying.

**Starter is model-starved** — no Veo 3.1, no Seedance 2.5, no Kling 2.5 Turbo — yet it is the tier most people start on. **Long-form stops at 20 minutes**, which disqualifies 30–60 minute compilation channels outright.

**Social proof is self-reported.** 4.8/5, "500+ five-star reviews" and "100K+ creators" appear on Vid.AI's own pages with no link to an independent platform; I could not verify them against G2, Capterra or Trustpilot.
