## What Fliki Actually Is

Fliki occupies a different slot from the avatar tools most people mean when they say "AI video." No talking head required. You paste a script, blog post, idea or slide deck, pick a voice, and the product assembles scenes from stock and AI-generated media with narration over the top. The output is the faceless video that fills YouTube explainer channels, course modules, product demos and localized marketing clips.

The feature list is broad for the price: 300 voices on the free tier rising to 2,000+ on Premium, 80+ languages, voice cloning, stock avatars, custom characters, a "Series" mode for repeatable formats, a translator, and YouTube publishing capped at 50 or 120 publications a month depending on tier.

Two design choices matter more than the feature list. **Fliki meters usage in credits, not seats** — every plan is "per user / month," but the real constraint is credits, and credits burn by the minute of finished audio or exported video. And **the free tier is a trial, not a tier**: 3 credits a month, roughly one short video. A fine demo, a poor substitute for a subscription — which is what you want if you are recommending it.

## The Credit Math: 180 Credits Is About Six Hours

Fliki's own estimator on the pricing page gives the conversion: **0.5 credits per minute**, inclusive of AI voiceover, assuming AI video and avatars appear in roughly 25% of runtime. A five-minute narrated video costs about **2.5 credits**.

| Plan | Credits/month | Minutes at 0.5 credits/min | Hours of finished video |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | 3 | ~6 min | 0.1 |
| Standard | 180 | ~360 min | **~6 hours** |
| Premium | 600 | ~1,200 min | **~20 hours** |

Two conclusions follow, and both matter for commission planning.

**The free tier is a single video.** Six minutes of finished output a month is enough to judge voice quality in your niche and little else. Free-to-paid conversion is therefore a real product decision rather than a trickle of tire-kickers — but it also happens fast or not at all.

**The export ceiling, not the credit count, forces the upgrade.** Standard caps exports at 15 minutes and 180 credits. A creator making 15-minute explainers spends 7.5 credits per video, so 24 videos exhausts the month. A team producing 40-minute training modules cannot use Standard at any credit level, because the export limit blocks it. The upgrade path is not "I ran out of credits" — it is "my format got longer." That is a more durable reason to pay, and a better angle to write about.

## The Strictest Advertising Rules in This Niche

The affiliate page says one line — "No paid ads or coupon/discount sites." The agreement says considerably more.

**Section 8.6** bans "any form of paid advertising (including but not limited to Pay-Per-Click, Pay-Per-Impression, social media ads, or banner ads)," with suspension without notice and forfeiture of pending commissions. That covers retargeting, boosted posts and display, not just search. **Section 8.2** bans bidding on Fliki brand terms, terminable "on the first occurrence." **Section 8.5** bars exclusive coupon sites. **Section 2.1.6** bars domains containing "Fliki."

The flip side matters just as much: **nothing restricts blog, review or SEO promotion.** That is not universal — HeyGen permits original video content only and rules out SEO/blog-only promotion, which is why that program does not appear on this site. Fliki's restrictions target paid traffic and coupon arbitrage, not organic reviews. For a search-driven site that works, provided distribution stays organic end to end.

The structural question is the cookie. Thirty days is the shortest window in the table above: StoryLab runs 90, Vid.AI and Zebracat run 60. For a product bought on impulse after a demo, 30 days is plenty. For a $252 annual decision made by a team comparing three tools, it is not — the reader who bookmarks your review in November and gets budget approval in January is invisible to you.

That changes how the content should be built, not whether it should be. Put the CTA where the decision happens, not only at the end: someone reading a pricing table is closer to buying than someone reading a conclusion 1,500 words later. And write for the same-session buyer — comparison queries convert inside the window, broad educational content does not.
