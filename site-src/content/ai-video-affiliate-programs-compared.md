## Why Cookie Windows Matter More Than Commission Rates

A commission rate tells you what you earn **if** a click converts. A cookie window tells you **how long you have** for that to happen.

For written content, that second number is the one that decides your income. Someone reading a 2,000-word review of an AI video tool is mid-research. They read three reviews, open a free account, test it for two weeks, get distracted, and subscribe a month later when a client project lands. A 30-day cookie loses that person. A 90-day cookie keeps them.

Video and social traffic behaves differently — the decision is minutes, not weeks — so a short cookie costs a YouTube affiliate far less than it costs a blogger.

This is why a 20% program with a 90-day window can outperform a 40% program with a 30-day window for a review site, and why the reverse is true for a channel that closes on impulse. There is no universally better program, only a better fit for your traffic.

## What "Lifetime" Actually Means

Every program here except Simplified claims lifetime or open-ended recurring commission. The word does enormous marketing work and almost no contractual work.

Lifetime commission means you are paid for as long as the customer stays subscribed — not for as long as the company exists, and not at a fixed rate forever. Standard affiliate agreements, including most on PartnerStack, Rewardful and Tolt, reserve the right to change commission rates with notice. "Lifetime" describes the customer relationship, not your rate.

Two practical consequences:

**Lifetime is worth less than it sounds, because churn is real.** A 30% lifetime commission on a customer who stays 14 months is worth about 4.2 months of revenue. Model it as a decaying annuity, not an asset.

**Lifetime is worth more on cheap products.** A $9/month customer at 20% pays you $1.80/month. Keeping them for three years is still only $65. A $45/month customer at the same rate pays $9/month. Product price dominates commission rate over any realistic horizon.

## The Traps

**Coupon and promo-code pages.** Zebracat prohibits them outright, with suspension as the penalty. Others restrict them in their terms. A page titled "BrandName Coupon Code 2026" is the single most common way affiliates lose accounts in this category, because those pages rank easily and convert well — right up until the ban.

**Brand bidding.** Bidding on a vendor's name or a misspelling in Google or Meta ads is prohibited by most programs here, and it is the fastest route to termination.

**Assuming published rates are current.** Two of the six programs in this table could not be verified at a vendor page. One publishes two different commission rates on two of its own pages. Rates change quietly, and directories do not always follow.

**Publishing a rate you have not seen.** If a review quotes a commission rate without a source, it was probably copied from another review. That is how the 30%/180-day figure for Picturesque keeps circulating when the vendor's own page says 20%.

## How to Verify Any Affiliate Program Yourself

Four checks, in this order:

1. **Find the affiliate page on the vendor's own domain.** Not a directory, not a roundup. If it 404s, that itself is information.
2. **Screenshot the terms with the date visible.** Rates change. Your screenshot is your evidence.
3. **Search the page for the word "cookie."** If it is absent, the program either does not publish it or hides it behind signup. Ask support in writing before you invest in content.
4. **Check the payout threshold conditions, not just the number.** Simplified's $50 minimum also requires two referrals — a detail that only appears in the FAQ.

If a vendor will not answer a direct question about cookie duration in writing, treat that as the answer.

## FAQ

**Which AI video affiliate program pays the most?**
AdCreative.ai reaches 40%, but only at the Gold tier above $5,000/month in referred revenue; the base is 30%. Vid.AI pays 33% flat. Zebracat pays 30% with the most clearly documented terms. The highest rate is not automatically the highest income.

**What is the best affiliate cookie duration?**
Longer is better for written content, where readers take weeks to convert. In this sample, StoryLab reports 90 days, Zebracat and Vid.AI report 60, and Simplified reports 30 or 45 depending on which of its own pages you read.

**Do these programs require a paid subscription to join?**
Zebracat is explicitly free to join. Picturesque's Creem page is a sign-in rather than an application, so there is no purchase gate. AdCreative requires an application with a strategy pitch, but not a purchase.

**Can I promote these tools on a blog?**
Zebracat names bloggers and review sites as accepted channels. Picturesque names "AI/tool reviewers." AdCreative asks for your strategy and links during approval. In all cases, paid ads, brand bidding and coupon sites are restricted or prohibited.

**Are lifetime commissions really lifetime?**
They run for as long as the customer stays subscribed. Rates themselves can be changed under most affiliate agreements, so treat "lifetime" as a statement about the customer relationship, not a guaranteed rate.
